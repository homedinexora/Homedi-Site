4. Consent — and How to Withdraw It

  • At onboarding we present a clear, itemised notice and request your consent. Consent is specific, informed, and given by clear affirmative action.
  • Withdrawal: You may withdraw consent at any time from Settings → Privacy & Data, or by writing to the contacts in Section 12. Withdrawal is as easy as giving consent. On withdrawal we will stop the related processing; the lawfulness of processing before withdrawal is unaffected. Some processing required by law (for example retention of medical or tax records) continues on that legal basis.
  • Withdrawing consent needed to operate your account (for example, phone-number processing) will mean we can no longer provide the service, and will be treated as a request to close the account.
  • Communication preferences: transactional messages (booking confirmations, OTPs, reminders, refund updates, safety notices) are part of the service and continue while your account is active. Promotional messages are sent only with your separate, optional consent, which you can withdraw at any time in Settings; we honour TRAI DND preferences for promotional content.
  • Once Consent Managers registered under the DPDP Rules are operational, we will support giving, managing, reviewing, and withdrawing consent through an interoperable Consent Manager platform.

5. ABHA and ABDM — Optional

  • Linking an ABHA is entirely optional. Discovery, booking, and prescriptions work identically without it. We never require ABHA at sign-up, discovery, or booking.
  • If you choose to link, verification happens through an OTP sent by ABDM/National Health Authority systems to your ABDM-registered mobile number. We then store your ABHA number (encrypted) and verification status.
  • If you have a linked, verified ABHA, prescriptions issued on the Platform can additionally be transmitted to your ABDM Personal Health Record as a structured FHIR document, under the ABDM consent framework and the ABDM Health Data Management Policy.
  • You may unlink ABHA at any time in Settings; we then stop pushing records to ABDM. Records already in your ABDM PHR are governed by ABDM and your PHR app.

6. Sharing of Personal Data

6.1 With your provider

When a booking is confirmed, the attending doctor or nurse receives the information needed to deliver the visit: patient name, age-relevant details, visit address, appointment details, and information you choose to share. Providers are independent professionals bound by professional secrecy, by the DPDPA, and by platform terms restricting use of your data to your care.

Records storage as a technology service: we store the visit records and prescriptions generated on the Platform on the basis of your consent, and make them available to your provider — including providers who do not maintain clinic premises of their own — solely for your care and their legal record-keeping obligations. Providers remain independent professionals: this storage does not make them our employees, and their access to your records is limited to your visits and recorded in our audit trail.

6.2 With your caregiver / for your dependent

A caregiver you (or your dependent, via OTP) have authorised can book visits and, only where the permission level allows, view the dependent's visit records and prescriptions. Caregiver links can be revoked at any time.

7. Data Residency and Security

  • All personal data is stored in India (AWS Mumbai region, ap-south-1). We will comply with any restrictions notified by the Central Government on cross-border transfer of personal data, including any applicable to Significant Data Fiduciaries.
  • Sensitive fields — including your phone number, name, date of birth, email, and ABHA number — are encrypted at the field level in our database; data in transit is protected by TLS.
  • Access to production data is role-restricted and logged in an append-only audit trail; audit archives are written to tamper-evident (write-once) storage.
  • Health records and prescriptions are stored in access-controlled storage; prescription documents are delivered through authenticated, time-limited links.
  • Application logs never contain names, phone numbers, health data, or OTPs — only internal identifiers.
  • OTP-based login is rate-limited; sessions use short-lived signed tokens that can be revoked on logout.

8. Data Retention and Erasure

  • We retain personal data only as long as necessary for the purposes stated, and then delete or anonymise it.
  • Medical records: visit records and prescriptions are retained for the period required of medical records under applicable law and professional regulations [typically at least 3 years for outpatient records per NMC norms; confirm with counsel], notwithstanding account deletion.
  • Account inactivity: in line with the DPDP Rules, if you neither use your account nor exercise your rights for the prescribed period [one year, per the DPDP Rules as applicable to us], we will erase your personal data, giving you at least 48 hours' prior notice to enable you to retain your account.
  • Erasure requests: when you request erasure (Section 9), we erase or anonymise your personal data and cause our processors to do the same, except where retention is required by law (for example medical-record, tax, or audit obligations) — in which case data is retained only for that purpose and then erased. Deletion is implemented so that erased records cannot be restored to active use.
  • Audit logs are retained for the period required to demonstrate compliance and investigate security incidents, and at least the periods prescribed under the DPDP Rules, then deleted.

9. Your Rights as a Data Principal

Under the DPDPA you have the following rights, exercisable free of charge through Settings → Privacy & Data or the contacts in Section 12:

  • Right to access: obtain a summary of your personal data being processed, the processing activities, and the identities of persons with whom it has been shared.
  • Right to correction and completion: have inaccurate or incomplete data corrected or updated.
  • Right to erasure: have your personal data erased, subject to legally required retention (Section 8).
  • Right of grievance redressal: raise a grievance with our Grievance Officer / Data Protection Officer and receive a response within the prescribed time.
  • Right to nominate: nominate a person who may exercise your rights in the event of your death or incapacity — particularly important for health records.
  • Right to withdraw consent at any time (Section 4).

We will verify your identity (via your registered phone number) before acting on a request. If you are not satisfied with our response, you may approach the Data Protection Board of India in the manner prescribed under the DPDPA.

10. Children and Persons Under Guardianship

  • The Platform is intended for use by adults (18+). A person under 18 cannot hold their own account; their care is arranged through a parent or lawful guardian using the caregiver facility.
  • Before processing a minor's personal data we obtain verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian in accordance with the DPDPA and DPDP Rules. The same applies to persons with disability who have a lawful guardian.
  • We do not undertake tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children. Processing of children's data is limited to the delivery of the booked healthcare service and related record-keeping, consistent with exemptions available for health services under the DPDP Rules.

11. Personal Data Breach

In the event of a personal data breach affecting you, we will notify you without delay through your registered communication channel, describing the nature of the breach, its likely consequences, the mitigation measures taken, and the steps you should take to protect yourself. We will also notify the Data Protection Board of India in the form and within the timelines prescribed by the DPDP Rules (initial intimation without delay, followed by a detailed report within 72 hours).
